WHAT THIS IS
Formly, "Stripe for forms": a business adds a Fill with Formly button; the user connects their accounts in a Formly popup, and the business gets back a filled form with only the answers the user approves. UW Hackathon, T-Mobile track. Team: Khangai Sukhbaatar, Salvo Bonsma, Jerry Zheng, Arjun Jindal. We pitch at a booth: 5 minutes per judge visit on a laptop, about 2 minutes of talk, the rest questions. The demo is a race: one person types T-Mobile's sign-up by hand while the judge taps Fill with Formly.

THE PITCH
Revenue for T-Mobile by removing sign-up friction. Not a privacy pitch; privacy only comes up if a judge asks (hidden slide 11). In the demo the Google connector is a mock stand-in for connecting accounts; that is intended.

NUMBERS (each appears once; never repeat a number or add weak ones)
On the T-Mobile slide, alternating percent and big number: 50% of Americans say switching hassle puts them off (YouGov, 2023); ~5M accounts sign up at T-Mobile every year (our estimate: 34.7M x 0.99% x 12 = ~4.1M churn + ~1M net adds, 2026 guidance); 10% more finishing (a conservative assumption); $940M more revenue a year (our math). Label estimates as ours.
Never invent a number or a source.
Autofill comparison (slide 7): on our 33-field T-Mobile form, browser autofill covers 13 fields (name, email, phone, address, card); Formly can fill 30; 3 are the customer's own choices (plan, lines, keep number). Don't claim Formly fills all 33.
Also used once each, on their own stats slides: 81% have abandoned an online form after starting it (The Manifest, 2018); 70% of online checkouts are abandoned (Baymard Institute, 2025); 68% of people dropped a financial sign-up with ID and credit checks last year (Signicat, Battle to Onboard 2022, Europe); the #1 reason a number transfer fails is the wrong account number (Gigs porting guide). Do not use autofill's 75% or 45% figures.
